Two ways to spend less time chasing statements and get more insight from them — one we can start today, one worth asking to unlock.

Why

The problem, plainly

Every month and quarter, our fund managers send account statements. They arrive in Canoe, the platform that collects our documents. Today, someone on the team logs each one by hand in a spreadsheet to keep track of what's come in and what's still missing.

It works — but it takes real time, it's easy to fall behind, and Canoe frequently mislabels which period a statement covers, so a person has to double-check. The goal here is to take that routine load off the team, not to change how they work.

What we're proposing

Two complementary tools

IDEA 1 · START NOW

Statement Tracker

A simple dashboard that watches Canoe for new statements and shows, at a glance, what's arrived, what's still outstanding, and what needs a closer look — organized by firm, fund, and period.

In plain terms: it does the routine tracking automatically and works alongside the spreadsheet — the team keeps the sheet, but spends far less time keeping it current.
✔ Fits our current policy
IDEA 2 · WORTH UNLOCKING

Document Intelligence

An optional next step that reads the statements themselves to pull out key figures — things like value, fees, and returns — and tracks how they move quarter over quarter and manager over manager, flagging what's worth a second look.

In plain terms: it turns a pile of PDFs into trends and early warnings. It uses AI to read the documents, so it needs a sign-off first (see below).
▲ Needs an AI-use approval
The one thing to decide

Where the AI policy comes in

Idea 1 is clear. Idea 2 needs a green light.

Idea 1 only reads Canoe's labels and dates — never the contents of the documents — so there's nothing new to approve. We can build it under today's rules.

Idea 2 has AI read the statements, which are sensitive financial documents. Our AI policy allows that only inside our licensed Microsoft environment, or with sign-off from the IT Lead/CTO. So the single decision that unlocks Idea 2 is that review — an approval call, not a technical one.

How we've scoped each idea

A "build now" and a "better" version of each

To make the choice easy, each idea is written up two ways:

Version 1 · Build now

Stays fully within today's policy. Nothing new to approve — we can start immediately.

Version 2 · Better, with approval

Does more or does it better, but needs the IT Lead/CTO sign-off first. The write-up explains why it's worth asking.

Cost stays low either way: the tools only do work when a new statement actually arrives, so there's no ongoing expense from letting them run.
